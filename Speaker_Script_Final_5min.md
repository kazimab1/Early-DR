# Final 5-minute presentation: speaking script

| # | Time | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:08 | Hi, my project is early detection of diabetic retinopathy from retinal photographs. |
| 2 | 0:22 | Let me start with why I chose this. The number of adults living with diabetes grew from 151 million in 2000 to 589 million in 2024. Each of them needs regular retinal screening, so the number of fundus images to grade keeps growing. |
| 3 | 0:35 | The screening pathway breaks down before an image is graded. About 60 percent of patients skip the annual exam, grading takes specialist time, and early disease has no symptoms. On the left is an advanced case, and on the right is how a patient with advanced diabetic retinopathy sees the world. Early disease is far harder to spot, which is why we need rapid, consistent triage right after the image is captured. |
| 4 | 0:25 | So the goal is a triage aid for the ophthalmologist, not a replacement. The model reads a fundus image and gives a calibrated probability of early disease, grades 1 and 2, at a fixed specificity, so high-risk images are seen first. |
| 5 | 0:35 | My overall question is: when datasets are merged for early-DR detection, does the model learn the disease or the source? I group it into two objectives. The first is the merge itself: does adding EyePACS help on unseen sources, and does a threshold set on validation survive a change of source? The second is image characteristics: which factors, such as camera type, image quality and lighting, differ between datasets and act as shortcuts, and can we reduce their influence so the model focuses on early signs of diabetic retinopathy and works across clinics and datasets? |
| 6 | 0:25 | Unlike approaches that primarily focus on detecting advanced diabetic retinopathy, my project targets early detection. I investigate whether merging retinal image datasets improves performance or introduces dataset-specific biases. I also evaluate how reliably the resulting models perform on unseen datasets. |
| 7 | 0:30 | We preprocess retinal images and feed them into EfficientNet-B0 to predict early diabetic retinopathy. We then calibrate the predicted probabilities and apply a predefined threshold to flag potentially affected patients. Finally, we evaluate the model on unseen datasets to determine how reliably it generalizes across different data sources. |
| 8 | 0:25 | Early DR is rare: grade 1 is only about 6 to 10 percent of the images, so a model can look accurate while ignoring it. I use three approaches. Sampling shows rare grades more often. Loss weighting gives them more weight. Augmentation adds variety, with a control to make sure it does not leak the label. Each is tested one at a time against plain training and judged on early-DR performance, not accuracy. |
| 9 | 0:25 | For data I use DDR, EyePACS split by patient, and APTOS as an external test only. The splits are frozen files, the model is EfficientNet-B0 at 512 pixels with three seeds on a Kaggle GPU, and test sets are opened only through a declared analysis. |
| 10 | 0:35 | Next steps: I will train two main arms. A uses DDR only, and B adds EyePACS with source balancing. Arm C is a separate control experiment: a deliberately flawed merge that shows whether a model learns the data source instead of the disease. It is a control, not a candidate model. I will handle imbalance, test on unseen sources with confidence intervals, and study how to identify and reduce the influence of camera type, image quality and lighting. The expected contribution is evidence and practical guidance on when merging retinal datasets helps or harms early detection. |
