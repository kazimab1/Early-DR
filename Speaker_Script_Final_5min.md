| # | Time | Say |

| 1 | 0:08 | Hi, my project is early detection of diabetic retinopathy from retinal photographs. |
| 2 | 0:22 | Let me start with why I chose this. The number of adults living with diabetes grew from 151 million in 2000 to 589 million in 2024. Each of them needs regular retinal screening, so the number of fundus images to grade keeps growing. |
| 3 | 0:35 | The screening pathway breaks down before an image is graded. About 60 percent of patients skip the annual exam, grading takes specialist time, and early disease has no symptoms. Compare the two photographs: on the right, grade 4 shows obvious bleeding and bright deposits; on the left, grade 1 is subtle and easy to miss. That early stage is the one I want to catch, which is why we need rapid, consistent triage right after the image is captured. |
| 4 | 0:15 | The animation starts with normal vision, then shows how a patient with advanced diabetic eye disease sees: dark patches and blur spread across the field of view. Early disease causes none of this, so patients do not seek care, which is why screening has to catch it early. |
| 5 | 0:25 | So the goal is a triage aid for the ophthalmologist, not a replacement. The model reads a fundus image and gives a calibrated probability of early disease, grades 1 and 2, at a fixed specificity, so high-risk images are seen first. |
| 6 | 0:35 | My overall question is: when datasets are merged for early-DR detection, does the model learn the disease or the source? I group it into two objectives. The first is the merge itself: does adding EyePACS help on unseen sources, and does a threshold set on validation survive a change of source? The second is image characteristics: which factors, such as camera type, image quality and lighting, differ between datasets and act as shortcuts, and can we reduce their influence so the model focuses on early signs of diabetic retinopathy and works across clinics and datasets? |
| 7 | 0:25 | Unlike approaches that primarily focus on detecting advanced diabetic retinopathy, my project targets early detection. I investigate whether merging retinal image datasets improves performance or introduces dataset-specific biases. I also evaluate how reliably the resulting models perform on unseen datasets. |
| 8 | 0:30 | We preprocess retinal images and feed them into EfficientNet-B0 to predict early diabetic retinopathy. We then calibrate the predicted probabilities and apply a predefined threshold to flag potentially affected patients. Finally, we evaluate the model on unseen datasets to determine how reliably it generalizes across different data sources. |
| 9 | 0:25 | Early DR is rare: grade 1 is only about 6 to 10 percent of the images, so a model can look accurate while ignoring it. We plan to compare three strategies one at a time against plain training: sampling, loss weighting and augmentation, with a control to make sure augmentation does not leak the label, and to judge each on early-DR detection rather than accuracy. We hope to find the strategy that best detects rare early-stage disease, or to show that plain training is already enough, and to give practical advice for training on imbalanced medical images. |
| 10 | 0:25 | For data I use three sources: DDR with 12,522 images, EyePACS with 35,108 split by patient, and APTOS with 3,662 as an external test only. Look at the grade mix: most images are healthy, early disease, grades 1 and 2, is 41 percent of DDR, 22 percent of EyePACS and 37 percent of APTOS, and severe grades are rare in all three. These differences are exactly why merging them needs care. |
| 11 | 0:35 | Next steps: I will train three arms. A trains on DDR, B on EyePACS, and C on the DDR plus EyePACS merge. Every arm is validated on a held-out part of its own training data, and APTOS is reserved for external testing only, alongside each source’s held-out test split. To tell more data apart from more diversity, I add a size-matched merge with the same number of training images as one source. I will also compare imbalance strategies and study how to reduce the influence of camera type, image quality and lighting, with confidence intervals. The expected contribution is evidence and practical guidance on when merging retinal datasets helps or harms early detection. |
