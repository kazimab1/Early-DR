# Final 5-minute presentation: speaking script

The same text is in the speaker notes of DR_Final_5min.pptx.

| # | Time | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:08 | Hi, my project is early detection of diabetic retinopathy from retinal photographs. |
| 2 | 0:22 | Let me start with why I chose this. The number of adults living with diabetes grew from 151 million in 2000 to 589 million in 2024. Each of them needs regular retinal screening, so the number of fundus images to grade keeps growing. |
| 3 | 0:25 | The screening pathway breaks down before an image is graded. About 60 percent of patients do not get an annual eye exam, grading takes specialist time, and early disease has no symptoms, so late detection lets preventable vision loss progress. |
| 4 | 0:25 | So the goal is a triage aid for the ophthalmologist, not a replacement. The model reads a fundus image and gives a calibrated probability of early disease, grades 1 and 2, at a fixed specificity, so high-risk images are seen first. |
| 5 | 0:50 | My main question is: when datasets are merged for early-DR detection, does the model learn the disease or the source? Merging is common, yet I found no study testing it. RQ1 asks whether adding EyePACS really helps, using a negative-control merge. RQ2 asks whether a threshold frozen on validation survives a change of source. RQ3 asks whether capture cues like image size are predicting the label. RQ4 asks which acquisition characteristics, such as image quality, camera type, illumination and resolution, must be controlled when datasets are merged, which improve generalisation, which degrade it, and what evidence-based guidance follows. RQ5 asks whether the influence of irrelevant characteristics, such as camera type, image quality and lighting, can be identified and reduced, so the model focuses on early signs of diabetic retinopathy and performs reliably across clinics and datasets. RQ4 and RQ5 together are the second main focus of the project. |
| 6 | 0:35 | How is this different? The common target is referable DR; mine is early DR at a fixed specificity. I test the merge instead of assuming it helps, I compare imbalance methods one at a time with a control, I test on held-out patients and an external set, and I report calibrated probabilities. It is not a new network: the value is the design and the evidence. |
| 7 | 0:30 | The model is simple. A fundus image is cropped, padded and resized to 512 by 512, passed through an EfficientNet-B0, then calibrated with temperature scaling and a prior-shift correction. The output is a probability of early disease and a flag. For example, a raw score of 0.88 becomes a calibrated 0.62, which is above the 0.30 threshold set on validation, so the image is flagged and sent for grading. The numbers are illustrative. |
| 8 | 0:30 | Early DR is rare. Grade 1 is only about 6 to 10 percent of the images, so a model can look accurate while ignoring early disease. I will handle this with sampling, such as a weighted sampler, class-aware batches and repeat-factor sampling; with loss functions, such as class-balanced cross-entropy, focal loss and label smoothing; and with augmentation, including a minority-only control for label leakage. A prior-shift correction keeps the probabilities honest after resampling. Each strategy is changed one at a time against plain cross-entropy and judged by early-DR PR-AUC and grade-1 sensitivity at a fixed specificity, not by accuracy. |
| 9 | 0:25 | For data I use DDR, EyePACS split by patient, and APTOS as an external test only. The splits are frozen files, the model is EfficientNet-B0 at 512 pixels with three seeds on a Kaggle GPU, and test sets are opened only through a declared analysis. |
| 10 | 0:35 | Next steps: train three arms with several seeds. A is DDR only. B is DDR plus all EyePACS grades, source-balanced. C is DDR plus only EyePACS grades 1, 3 and 4, an old merge kept as a negative control. Then handle imbalance one strategy at a time, test on unseen sources with confidence intervals, report sensitivity at a fixed specificity, and, for RQ4, study image quality, camera type and lighting one factor at a time, and for RQ5 test colour, brightness and size normalisation and matched subsets. |
| 11 | 0:03 | These are my references. |
| 12 | 0:03 | Thank you for listening. |
